# Dungeoneer — The Six Shattered Domains

*The collapsed worlds built by The Severed after they killed The Unnamed. Each one is a different answer to the question: what did you want different to mean? Each answer was coherent. Each answer failed. The Dungeon is their sediment.*

---

## I. The Bound — Domain of Law

**The Severed:** Solen, called The Lawbringer
**Dungeon Biome:** Catacomb
**Living Inheritor:** Varn, The Chainkeeper

### The Philosophy
Solen's grievance with the Unnamed was not with order itself — it was with *unearned* order. The Unnamed gave every soul purpose without asking. Solen wanted order that souls chose: laws they argued over, chains they accepted, a society that functioned because its members consented to function within it. He believed the difference between divine mandate and civil law was the difference between a cage and a home.

### At Its Peak
Chains were sacred objects — not instruments of imprisonment but of contract. You bound yourself to an oath, and the chain made it real and visible and permanent. Architecture was geometric, deliberate, legible — every building communicated its purpose. The society functioned with a precision no other world matched. Inequality existed but was codified. You could challenge it. People did. The law was argued, not merely obeyed.

### The Collapse
Law without wisdom is mechanics. When Solen's shard-power waned, the laws kept executing themselves. The system had no capacity to know when to bend. Those who questioned were punished. Those who enforced became the law itself. The chains meant to bind agreements began binding people — not to oaths they chose, but to roles they were born into. The society didn't burn. It suffocated, slowly, under the weight of its own perfect structure.

### What Survived
The Catacomb biome — ordered stone corridors, chain-decorated walls, geometric rooms built for purposes that no longer exist. Varn survived: a senior enforcer of the chain-laws, still carrying the ideology intact, still certain the chains were not the problem.

### Artifacts of The Bound
Items of consequence and mutual binding. Equipment that applies effects to both the wearer and the struck simultaneously. You take the debuff; they take the debuff. Legendary pieces make this asymmetric in the wielder's favor, but only barely — Solen's philosophy always believed in proportionality. The most powerful Bound artifacts impose law-like structures on combat: conditional effects that trigger on specific transgressions, chains that bind enemy abilities.

## II. The Pyre — Domain of Renewal

**The Severed:** Ciara, called The Brighthand
**Dungeon Biome:** Gehenna
**Living Inheritor:** Seris, The Ember

### The Philosophy
Ciara watched the Unnamed hold everything in eternal stasis and couldn't bear it. Nothing old made room for new. Suffering that should have ended was preserved alongside joy. She wanted a world that *renewed* — where death cleared ground for life, where fire served as mechanism rather than metaphor. She did not want destruction. She wanted the version of the world that comes after destruction, and she believed you couldn't have one without the other.

### At Its Peak
The Pyre was built to burn. Cities were constructed with renewal in mind — designed to be razed and rebuilt differently, each iteration learning from the last. Knowledge was preserved in seed-form rather than monuments, carried in people rather than stone. The inhabitants were genuinely comfortable with impermanence in a way that made them adaptable, honest about mortality, and philosophically clear-eyed in ways other civilizations found unsettling.

### The Collapse
The shard demanded faster cycles than the civilization could sustain. Power in The Pyre came through destruction — the shard activated on it. The most powerful people became those who destroyed most. Eventually the burning outpaced the rebirth. The ash had no seeds. The last cycle never finished. The world didn't die in war or external failure — it burned itself to nothing because the thing it had decided was virtuous had an appetite it couldn't control.

### What Survived
The Gehenna biome — fire-scorched ruins, eternal embers, burned architecture with new growth forcing itself through the cracks. Seris survived because she maintained the distinction: fire *serves* renewal, it does not replace it. Whether she can keep holding it — in a place with no renewal cycle, where the clearing never gives way to what grows after — is the question her arc is built around.

### Artifacts of The Pyre
Items that strengthen as the wielder takes damage. The closer to death, the more power available. Legendary Pyre artifacts convert being struck into offensive force — the hit that should have killed you instead feeds the next attack. The most aggressive build archetype in the game. High risk, transformative reward.

## III. The Veil — Domain of Selfhood

**The Severed:** Thessaly, called The Unmasked
**Dungeon Biome:** The Mirror Halls *(shifting architecture, illusion-heavy rooms)*
**Living Inheritor:** Mira, The Veil

### The Philosophy
Thessaly's quarrel with the Unnamed was precise: *truth should not be imposed.* Every soul in the Unnamed's order knew its place, its meaning, its end — not through discovery but through dictation. Identity, form, gender, role, beauty — all of it fixed before the soul had any say. Thessaly wanted sovereignty over selfhood. She wanted a world where the story you told about yourself was as ontologically real as any received truth. She wanted meaning to be *made*, not handed down.

### At Its Peak
The Veil was a civilization where form was chosen — not performed, not costumed, but genuinely chosen and made real by Thessaly's shard. Gender, appearance, name, identity — all fluid, all solid in the moment of choosing. The shard made chosen selfhood as ontologically real as any fixed truth. Someone who chose to be beautiful was beautiful in every sense that mattered. Someone who chose to be something the Unnamed's order had no category for simply *was* that thing, and the world accommodated it.

This produced a civilization of extraordinary intimacy, genuine philosophical complexity, and incomparable art. Questions other worlds never had to ask — *what is a self, what makes a person real, what do we owe each other when we are all becoming* — were the central problems of daily life. The arguments were fierce. The bonds were deep. The cruelty, when it came, was intimate in ways other worlds' cruelties weren't.

### The Collapse
Irreconcilable selves could not survive contact with each other. Each person's constructed reality was as ontologically real as anyone else's, and when two truths were fundamentally incompatible, one had to unmake the other to persist. Not through violence — through the simple act of asserting reality more forcefully. The war of selves was invisible and total. Eventually there was no shared ground left. The world dissolved — not into chaos, but into *fog*. Each soul in a pocket of their own truth, with nothing shared left to stand on.

### What Survived
A biome of shifting architecture where things genuinely are not what they appear — not as a trick, but because the world here *remembers* that appearance and reality were once the same thing. Mira survived because she holds one absolute distinction: the difference between a useful fiction and a destructive one. She applies this distinction on behalf of others, constantly, without being asked, and entirely on her own authority.

### Artifacts of The Veil
Items that operate through deception and misperception. They alter what enemies believe is happening — false positions, phantom images, redirected hit detection. Legendary Veil artifacts are the most mechanically complex items in the game. They impose false information at a deep level: an enemy that believes you are somewhere you are not will attack where you were. They reward players who understand exactly what they are doing.

## IV. The Canopy — Domain of Nature

**The Severed:** Aldren, called The Rootspeaker
**Dungeon Biome:** Moss
**Living Inheritor:** Kael, The Root

### The Philosophy
Aldren believed the Unnamed's order was artificial hierarchy imposed on a world that already had its own — natural, earned, honest. Strong survives. Weak is consumed. From that competition emerges genuine balance. He wanted to remove God's thumb from the scale and trust that what emerged from honest competition would be more true than anything divinely arranged.

### At Its Peak
The Canopy was the most physically beautiful of the six worlds — a vast living civilization grown rather than built. Living wood and cultivated stone. Hierarchy was real but earned: strength, cunning, endurance. The society was brutal in ways it never tried to hide. It was honest. You knew where you stood and what it would cost you to rise. The people who thrived there were genuinely formidable in every sense — physically, intellectually, ecologically. It was the only civilization that never lied about what it was.

### The Collapse
Competition without any organizing principle doesn't produce balance — it produces dominance. The thing that eventually won was not the strongest or most cunning. It was the most *consumptive*. The ecosystem hit a singularity: one force ate everything else and then starved. Aldren had trusted natural balance to self-correct. It didn't. The lesson Kael drew: balance requires a gardener. The gardener must be willing to prune.

He has been applying this lesson in the Dungeon ever since, with increasing willingness and decreasing hesitation about what qualifies for pruning.

### What Survived
The Moss biome — nature reclaiming ruins, overgrowth forcing itself through stone, things consumed and transformed by organic processes. Beautiful and suffocating simultaneously. Kael moves through it as if it belongs to him, and in a real sense it does.

### Artifacts of The Canopy
Items that scale with run duration. Weak at the start of a run, formidable by the end. Legendary Canopy artifacts create symbiotic effects — equipment that feeds on other equipment, buffs that strengthen adjacent buffs, items that grow roots into a build the longer they're worn. The highest ceiling of any artifact set, but only for players who survive long enough to realize it.

## V. The Pale — Domain of Preservation

**The Severed:** Vael, called The Stillkeeper
**Dungeon Biome:** Cocytus
**Living Inheritor:** None. The Pale's ideology died with its world.

### The Philosophy
Vael wanted stillness — not death, but the ability to hold perfection without watching it be consumed by time. The Unnamed's wheel kept turning. Everything moved toward its end. Vael wanted to stop the wheel for the things worth keeping.

### At Its Peak
The Pale preserved everything. Art, memory, experience, people — crystallized and held in forms that did not decay. It was the most complete archive of existence ever assembled. Nothing of value was ever lost. The civilization knew its own history with perfect fidelity, could access any moment of its past, could hold a beautiful thing indefinitely without it aging.

### The Collapse
Entropy is not defeated by stillness. It is delayed. When Vael's shard finally failed, everything he had preserved collapsed simultaneously — a civilization's worth of accumulated decay, arriving at once. What survived in the Dungeon are the vessels with nothing left inside. Perfect containers. Empty.

### What Survived
The Cocytus biome — frozen architecture, preservation cases that once held things, ruins of extraordinary beauty with nothing animate inside. The enemies here are the mechanisms of preservation still running — still reaching for things to hold, having forgotten that holding is not the same as living.

### Artifacts of The Pale
Items that freeze states — debuffs, cooldowns, enemy actions, status effects. Legendary Pale artifacts can hold a moment of combat in suspension: an attack that was about to land, a spell that was mid-cast, a killing blow that arrives one full second late. Time-manipulation mechanics that reward precise positioning and planning.

## VI. The Endless — Domain of Desire

**The Severed:** Carath, called The Hungering
**Dungeon Biome:** The Hollow Courts
**Living Inheritor:** None. The Endless consumed its own inheritors.

### The Philosophy
Carath's quarrel with the Unnamed was psychological. The Unnamed's order gave every soul exactly what it needed — no more, no less. Nobody went hungry. Nobody yearned. Carath believed that yearning — desire, hunger, the ache for something beyond what you have — was the engine of everything worth anything. Love. Ambition. Creation. Grief. You cannot love what you cannot lose. You cannot make something without wanting it to exist before it does. The Unnamed had removed the wanting, and Carath considered this an annihilation dressed as generosity.

### At Its Peak
The Endless produced extraordinary things precisely because its people wanted them. Art made from genuine desire rather than assigned purpose. Bonds formed from choice rather than function. Discovery driven by hunger for understanding. The civilization was loud, generative, sometimes violent, deeply alive. Carath believed this was what freedom actually felt like — not the absence of constraint, but the presence of wanting.

### The Collapse
Desire without limit is appetite without bottom. Each soul's hunger eventually exceeded what the world could provide. When the external world was exhausted, the only remaining resource was each other — their achievements, their power, their identity, their accumulated shard-fragments. The civilization didn't collapse from external failure or philosophical error. It ate itself from the inside out, soul by soul, each one believing their hunger was the justified kind.

### What Survived
A biome of beautiful ruins picked completely clean. What the Endless left behind is appetite without object — enemies that consume without knowing why, architecture that communicates abundance from which every actual thing has been stripped. The danger here is not violence. It is infectious hunger.

### Artifacts of The Endless
Items that feed on consumption. They grow stronger with kills, with mana drained, with health stolen. Legendary Endless artifacts absorb properties from what they destroy — a weapon that kills enough fire-aligned enemies begins to carry fire; armor worn through enough deaths begins to remember them and use them. The most dangerous artifact set to build around, because they require continuous feeding. Stop consuming and they weaken. Keep consuming and you become something the Dungeon recognizes.
