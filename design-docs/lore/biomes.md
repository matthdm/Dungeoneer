# Dungeoneer — Biome Environmental Descriptions

*What the player experiences in each biome. The architecture is not decoration — it is sediment. Every texture is what a civilization left behind when it finished collapsing.*

---

## Domain Biomes

These six biomes are the remnants of collapsed worlds. The deeper the floor within a biome, the denser the shard-residue in the walls. The enemies here are what the inhabitants became.

---

### The Catacomb — Domain of The Bound

**Textures:** `catacomb`, `crypt`, `normal`

The walls are dressed stone — cut by hands that believed precision was a form of respect. Every block is the same size. Every mortar line is the same width. The geometry is deliberate in a way that most architecture isn't, because in The Bound, architecture communicated intention. A building that couldn't be read was a building that hadn't been thought through.

The Catacomb is the most orderly of the Dungeon's biomes and also the most suffocating. The corridors are wide enough to move through comfortably and not an inch wider. The rooms are sized for their function. Chain-brackets still hang from walls that no longer hold chains, because no one removed them when they became decorative, because the law required they remain until formally decommissioned, and the decommissioning process was never completed before the world ended.

What the player notices, walking through long enough: the Catacomb has a logic. Not a hostile logic — a bureaucratic one. Rooms connect in ways that made sense when the system was running. The dead ends were not mistakes. They were checkpoints. The player is moving through a process that has no beginning and no end anymore.

The floor tile is dark grey stone with fine mortar. The doors have brackets for locks that are no longer installed. The walls remember what they were holding in place.

**What the enemies are:** Former inhabitants of The Bound who have lost the thread of the law but retained the shape of enforcement. They patrol. They do not know why. They respond to transgression — movement in unauthorized areas, approach without proper deference — the way any mechanism responds to incorrect input: with consequence.

---

### Gehenna — Domain of The Pyre

**Texture:** `gehena`

The walls are burning from the inside. This is not metaphor and not decay — it is a structural feature of what The Pyre built. The architecture was designed to be consumed and rebuilt. The fire is baked into the stone because the stone was made to hold fire and release it when the cycle came. The cycle came. It kept coming. It hasn't stopped.

The dark blocks glow red-orange at every crack and seam, as if the walls contain a heat that is always just below the surface, always pressing outward. The floor is hot. Not painfully — warmly, the way a hearth is warm. The Pyre at its height was comfortable like this. You knew what was going to happen. You had accepted it. The warmth was a promise that the cycle would be honored.

Now the warmth is just warmth. The promise it was attached to is not being kept. The fire that bleeds through the walls is not going anywhere. It is not clearing space for something better. It is simply burning, because the mechanism that ignited it never learned how to stop.

The architecture still suggests purpose — doors, corridors, the outlines of rooms that were built for functions the civilization cared about. The fire does not discriminate between them.

**What the enemies are:** Souls who were close to the shard when the final cycle ran and were... changed by it. They carry heat. They generate it. They have been in the burning long enough that the burning is now their natural state. They are not malicious. They are on fire. There is a difference.

---

### The Moss — Domain of The Canopy

**Texture:** `moss`

The walls are not walls. They are living things that happen to be providing structural support. The Canopy grew its civilization rather than built it, and what remains in the Dungeon is growth that has continued without any gardener to direct it. The corridors are tunnels through organic matter. The rooms are clearings.

The green is overwhelming and beautiful. It presses in from every surface — not aggressively, not hungrily, just insistently, the way life insists. Everything here is competing for the same light, the same space, the same nutrients in the sediment. The things that win are the things that are best at winning. The things that lose become part of the substrate.

There is no dead matter in the Moss. The Canopy did not produce dead matter — it produced consumed matter. What falls becomes what ate it. The floor is soft in a way that is slightly unsettling if you think about it.

Kael moves through this biome as though it recognizes him. It does. The shard-residue in the organic matter responds to what remains of The Canopy's organizing principle in his form. The moss parts slightly for him. It does not do this for the player. The player is assessed, like everything else, by what they are doing to the ecosystem they are moving through.

**What the enemies are:** Creatures that were part of The Canopy's natural hierarchy and have continued being part of it without the hierarchy to give them context. They occupy the place in the food chain they have always occupied. They do not understand that the food chain no longer exists as a system. They are doing exactly what they always did and calling it life.

### Cocytus — Domain of The Pale

**Texture:** `cocutos`

The walls are pale stone with fracture lines running through them like a map of every stress they have ever been under. The fractures are not structural failures — they are preservation vessel failures. The Pale filled its architecture with memory, with experience, with things worth keeping. When Vael's shard collapsed and all that accumulated decay arrived at once, the walls cracked under it.

The result is stone that looks like it survived something terrible while holding very still. The fractured crystalline texture catches light in ways that suggest depth — like the walls are not solid but layered, each layer a different thing that was held here before the holding failed. Whatever was inside has gone. The containers remain, perfect and empty.

The temperature is wrong. Not cold exactly — still, in the way that things that have not moved in a very long time are still. The Pale was built around the concept of perfect preservation, and what persists in the biome is the quality of preservation itself, stripped of anything to preserve. The player moves through it and feels, faintly, the specific texture of something missing.

**What the enemies are:** The mechanisms of preservation, still running. The Pale built elaborate systems to hold and maintain its archive. Those systems have no object to protect. They have not stopped trying. They reach for things to hold — and when the only thing present is the player, the reaching is directed accordingly.

---

### The Mirror Halls — Domain of The Veil

**Textures:** `lapis`, `pandem1`

The walls are deep blue with crystalline patterns that catch and scatter light in ways that are slightly inconsistent. The angle you saw a reflection from a moment ago is not the angle you see it from now. The walls are not lying — they are accurate to the physics of the domain they come from, where appearance and reality were the same thing, where a chosen truth was as real as any other. In the Mirror Halls, the light remembers that rule even though the shard that made the rule is gone.

The effect is subtle. Experienced players learn to navigate it. New players are confused in a specific way: not lost, exactly, but uncertain about which version of the room they are standing in. Both versions are partially real. The one the player commits to becomes more real than the other. This is also, perhaps, the most honest summary of Thessaly's philosophy available in physical form.

The blue is rich and full, the kind of blue that implies depth even on a flat surface. There is something restful about it. Mira's biome feels safer than it is, because Mira's biome feels like it is trying to accommodate you — like it is showing you a version of itself you can accept. What it is actually showing you is the version that benefits from your not looking too closely.

**What the enemies are:** Souls from The Veil who were in the middle of constructing or deconstructing a self when the world dissolved. They are not fully coherent. They do not know what they are. They react to the player as a mirror reacts — by showing back something, imperfectly. Combat with them is uncertain in a specific way: they appear to be where they are not.

---

### The Hollow Courts — Domain of The Endless

**Texture:** `gallery`

The walls were beautiful once. You can still see it — the architecture is sophisticated, the proportions are right, the design communicates ambition and confidence. What is harder to see, at first, is what happened to everything that was in them. The Hollow Courts are beautiful ruins that have been picked completely clean.

The red-brown veins running through the dark stone are not decoration. They are what the appetite left behind — the traces of consumption that ran through every surface, every room, every structure until there was nothing left to consume and the veins themselves became the only feature. They grew through the architecture the way roots grow through ground, and they found everything, and they took it, and now the architecture persists as a record of how thoroughly it was eaten.

The light in the Hollow Courts has a quality of abundance that does not match the emptiness. The proportions of the rooms suggest they were built to hold things. The things are gone. The suggestion remains, which is the worst way to experience abundance — to be in a space that was designed for more than is in it.

**What the enemies are:** Appetite without object. They consume because they were consuming when their world ended and they have continued without the world. Some of them have been at it long enough that the consumption has developed its own logic — preferences, patterns, something almost like taste. This is not reassuring. It means the hunger has had time to become something more.

## Deep Biomes — Non-Civilization Floors

*These biomes do not belong to any collapsed domain. They are what grows in the Dungeon itself, in the absence of any organizing principle except Abaddon's patient dissolution. They appear on deeper floors and in transitions between domain layers.*

*Domain artifacts do not generate here. What generates here is older.*

---

### Raw Stone — Pre-Civilization Layer

**Texture:** `rock`

Brown-orange unworked stone, the substrate beneath everything. No mortar lines, no dressed faces, no deliberate geometry. This is not a biome in the sense that the domain floors are biomes — it is the Dungeon's own material, the sediment that exists between layers of civilization.

Moving through raw stone floors is like moving through the gap between thoughts — the space where one civilization's sediment ends and another's has not yet begun. The enemies here are not remnants of ideology. They are things that live in stone the way organisms live in deep earth: by eating it, slowly, for very long periods of time.

The player who has been paying attention will notice that the raw stone floors feel different from the domain biomes. Quieter. Less purposeful. The domain biomes carry the weight of what they were. The raw stone carries nothing except depth.

---

### The Carved Ways — Creature Biome

**Texture:** `tunnel`

Something dug this. The rounded walls, the organic curves, the absence of any right angles — this was not constructed by anything with geometry in mind. It was *burrowed*. The dark reddish-brown is soil and compressed sediment and older stone broken apart and pushed to the sides by something large enough to reshape the Dungeon's substrate with its body.

The Carved Ways appear at depth, where the shard-residue is dense enough that prolonged exposure has produced things that were never souls and never civilization. They grew from the Dungeon's material, have been here since before the oldest domain layer finished collapsing, and have had long enough to make their own passages.

What lives here has never known anything except the Dungeon. It does not have philosophy. It does not have ideology. It has hunger, which it has refined over geological time into something very precise.

---

### The Hive and Lair Floors — Built by Appetite

**Textures:** `hive`, `lair`

Yellow-amber organic structures that were not grown and not built but accumulated — layer after layer of the same biological material compacted into walls and floors and ceilings by something that returned here often enough and long enough that the returning became architecture.

The hive floors feel inhabited in a way that makes the player aware they are inside something rather than moving through it. The warmth here is different from Gehenna's — biological, not fiery. The walls are slightly soft. Things have been living here.

These floors carry the residue of whatever The Endless produced when its consumption had no more civilization to eat. The appetite turned inward and then sideways, onto the Dungeon itself, and the Dungeon absorbed enough of it that these floors now grow their own version of the hunger — not ideological, not shard-powered, just old and fed and patient.

---

### The Mucus Floor — Pure Dissolution

**Texture:** `mucus`

Bright, wet, biological green. Not a domain. Not a creature habitat. The Dungeon's own dissolution process, visible and physical. Abaddon receives everything eventually — the Mucus floors are what that process looks like when it is close to the surface, when the absorption is active rather than geological.

The player who reaches Mucus floors is deep. Very deep. The shard-residue on these floors is ancient — pre-Severance layers, dissolved domains so old their domain cannot be identified. The enemies here are not remnants of anything recognizable. They are the dissolution itself given enough structure to interact with.

---

### The Crimson Deep — Azazel's Sediment

**Texture:** `pandem2`

Deep red, almost blood-colored, with a sparkling crystalline quality that implies depth that is not actually there. These floors appear only in the deepest accessible runs, at the layer where Azazel's presence is most accumulated.

The crimson soaked into the stone over the length of every consequence Azazel has applied in these depths since the Severance. It is not actual blood. It is the residue of judgment — thousands of years of consequence applied without calibration, soaking into the architecture the way grief soaks into wood. The sparkle is the shard-residue in old divine authority, scattered and pointless and still faintly glowing.

Moving through the Crimson Deep, the player feels assessed. Not threatened — assessed. Whatever is looking does not have hostile intent. It has the only intent it has ever had: to determine whether what is present has transgressed, and to apply consequence accordingly. The calibration has not improved with time.
