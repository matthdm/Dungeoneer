# Dungeoneer — Minor NPCs

*The Dungeon contains souls who are not major arcs — not ascending, not building toward anything, not enemies. They are simply present. They have been here a long time. Each of them is a window into a world that ended.*

---

## The Hollow Monk

**Sprite:** Oracle
**Domain of Origin:** The Pale
**Location:** Cocytus biome, sanctuary rooms

The Hollow Monk was a member of a preservation order — a monastic community whose entire practice was devoted to maintaining the stillness Vael's shard required. The order performed rituals of preservation: the same prayers at the same times, the same movements, the same formal observances, cycle after cycle. The rituals were not spiritual in the conventional sense. They were functional. They kept the archive stable. Without the rituals, the crystalline preservation structures would develop micro-fractures. The order was a maintenance crew dressed as a religious institution, and they knew it, and they honored both descriptions equally.

The archive is gone. The Monk performs the rituals anyway.

Not because they believe the archive still exists. Not out of habit in the simple sense. They perform the rituals because stopping would require deciding what to do instead, and deciding has become impossible. The Monk has been in the Dungeon long enough that the rituals are the entire remaining structure of their identity. Without them, there is nothing to be. So the rituals continue: the same prayers, the same movements, the same times, in a ruin of pale stone that no longer holds anything.

**What they offer:** The Monk is calm in a way that is unlike anything else in the Dungeon. They do not react to the player's presence with suspicion or urgency. They finish the ritual they are performing before they speak. When they do speak, they speak in the formal register of the preservation order — precise, unhurried, using the specific vocabulary of archival work. They will share fragments of what the archive held: history, taxonomy, records of the other domains from The Pale's perspective. The records are partially corrupted — the Monk's memory has developed its own micro-fractures, and some of what they report is wrong in ways they cannot identify. What is right is genuinely valuable. What is wrong sounds indistinguishable from what is right.

**Key line:** *"The third observance is the anchor of the day. I have not yet determined what the day is anchored to, now. But the third observance holds the form regardless."*

## The Forgotten Soldier

**Sprite:** GreyKnight
**Domain of Origin:** The Bound
**Location:** Catacomb biome, corridor intersections

The Forgotten Soldier was stationed at a perimeter post on the outer boundary of The Bound's territory — a rotating assignment, meant to last one cycle before relief came and the next soldier took the position. The relief never came. The world ended. The Soldier is still at the post.

They have been at the post long enough that they have stopped expecting the relief. They have stopped expecting anything. What remains is the post — the specific intersection they are responsible for, the patrol pattern they were assigned, the formal greeting they deliver to anyone who approaches from the direction of The Bound's interior. They deliver it to the player. They deliver it to walls. They have been delivering it since before the player can imagine.

They are not hostile. They will not attack unless their post is directly threatened — and the definition of direct threat is precisely what they were trained to recognize, nothing more. They enforce their assigned boundary with the same completeness they always did. The boundary is a corridor in a ruin. This does not change the enforcement.

If you ask them to leave: *"I have not been relieved."*

If you ask them how long they have been here: A long pause. Then: *"The relief comes at the end of the cycle. I have not yet determined the current length of this cycle."*

**What they offer:** Information about The Bound's structure — the formal hierarchy, the names and roles of the institutions they served. Some of this is now only available through the Soldier, because the Catacomb's records have deteriorated past legibility. They do not know this. They deliver institutional information with the confidence of someone reporting to a superior, not the care of someone preserving history.

They will also, if the player sits with them long enough in the correct formal register, relax slightly. Not out of character — still procedurally correct — but the version of them that exists underneath the post emerges briefly. It was a person, once. It had preferences. It is still in there, somewhere behind the relief that never came.

**Key line:** *"This position has been held continuously for—"* A pause that goes on too long. *"—continuously."*

## The Weeping Shade

**Sprite:** Tortured Soul
**Domain of Origin:** The Veil
**Location:** Mirror Halls biome, dead-end rooms

There is no name for what the Weeping Shade is, because the Weeping Shade no longer has a name. They lost it in the war of selves. Not violently — they encountered someone who held a more complete truth and their constructed self dissolved around them, the way fog dissolves in morning light. They were not destroyed. They are still here. They are the afterimage of a self that was held and then wasn't.

They weep because grief is the last thing that persists when everything else is dissolved. Not the memory of what was lost — the sensation of having lost something, without the ability to remember what. The loss is felt completely. The lost thing is not accessible.

They do not harm the player. They are not capable of it. When the player approaches, they do not react with fear or welcome — they react with the kind of looking that happens when you are not sure if what you are seeing is real. The Shade has been uncertain about what is real for a very long time.

**What they offer:** Fragments. When the player stays near the Weeping Shade long enough, pieces of the lost self surface — brief images, snatches of dialogue, glimpses of who this person was before they lost. The Shade cannot access these themselves. The player receives them as environmental lore: pieces of a life in The Veil, what it was like to be becoming, the specific moment when the becoming stopped.

The fragments are not organized. They do not form a complete picture. They are the shards of a person, rising to the surface when something coherent is nearby, then sinking again when it passes.

**Key line:** None. The Weeping Shade does not speak. They make a sound that is almost language and is not.

## The Scavenger

**Sprite:** RockCollector
**Domain of Origin:** The Endless
**Location:** Hollow Courts biome, anywhere items are present

The Scavenger was a child when The Endless collapsed. Too young to have internalized the ideology, too young to have participated in the final consumption, old enough to have survived it by doing the only thing that worked: taking what was available before someone else did. They are not an advocate for desire as the engine of creation. They are not a philosopher of any kind. They just survived, and they are still surviving, by the same method.

They will trade with the player. They will also steal from the player if the player is careless — not as an attack, not out of hostility, simply because an unattended item is an available item and available items are taken. This is not a moral position. It is not even a choice. It is reflex built by decades of survival in the Hollow Courts, where anything not actively held was consumed.

They are the most practical person in the Dungeon. They want things. They say so. They ask for things directly. If you have something they want, they tell you what they will give for it. If the trade is refused, they will try again later when conditions have changed. They do not hold grudges because holding grudges is not useful.

If you ask them about their world: *"It ended. I didn't."* 

If you press: *"That's the whole of it. Everything else is detail."*

They have more detail than they are sharing. They share it in portions, over multiple interactions, each piece in exchange for something. The detail, accumulated, is a picture of The Endless at its end — from the perspective of a child who watched without understanding and understood more than they should have.

**Key line:** *"That's useful to you. What's it worth to you? Be specific — I don't do favors and I don't take compliments."*

## The Mad Scholar

**Sprite:** BlueChampion
**Domain of Origin:** The Pale (primarily), with cross-domain contamination
**Location:** Any biome — they move, they explore, they cannot stop

The Mad Scholar was an archivist. A very good one — someone who had memorized significant portions of The Pale's accumulated knowledge because the preservation vessels occasionally needed restoration, and restoration required knowing what should be in them. They held more of the archive in their head than any single vessel held in crystal. They were proud of this. They were right to be proud of this.

The collapse scrambled it. The transition to the Dungeon disrupted the ordering principles that allowed the memorized material to be accurate and accessible. The archive is still in there — the Scholar can feel it, all of it, the weight of ten thousand records pressing against the inside of their head — but the indexing is gone. They cannot retrieve a specific fact. They receive them in uncontrolled bursts, mixed with their own synthesis, mixed with things they have invented to fill gaps that the synthesis requires, mixed with things they have simply been in the Dungeon long enough to have accumulated from other sources.

They know that some of what they know is wrong. They do not know which parts. Everything feels equally certain. They have stopped trying to distinguish between the certain-and-correct and the certain-and-fabricated because the distinction is not accessible and the trying is exhausting.

They talk constantly. They offer information to everyone they encounter because the offering provides some relief from the pressure of holding all of it. They cannot guarantee what is accurate. They say so, once, at the beginning. Then they continue talking.

**What they offer:** The highest-variance information source in the game. The Mad Scholar can tell the player things about the domains, the cosmology, the NPC histories that are not available anywhere else — and can also confidently tell the player things that are completely wrong. Some of the wrong things are interesting wrong things: syntheses that didn't happen, histories that could have happened, interpretations of actual events that are incorrect and also illuminate something the correct account misses. The player must decide what to trust.

The Mad Scholar has been to the deep floors. They have encountered Azazel. They have more direct knowledge of Abaddon's ambient presence than anyone else in the Dungeon, because they went looking. They report all of this with the same confidence with which they report everything else.

**Key line:** *"That's — yes, I know that. I know that. That was recorded in the fifteenth archive of the third Pale preservation cycle — or possibly I'm thinking of the account of the Bound's seventh constitutional revision, the two are adjacent in my memory for reasons I've never been able to reconstruct. The substance is accurate. Probably. What did you want to know?"*
