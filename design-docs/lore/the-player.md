# Dungeoneer — The Player's Nature

*Why do you come back?*

*This document is an internal consistency reference, not player-facing lore. It answers questions the game poses but never fully resolves. Use it to ensure that all hints, inscriptions, NPC observations, and NG+ reveals are pointing at the same thing.*

---

## The Central Mystery

Every soul in the Dungeon dissolves eventually. Abaddon's process is slow but comprehensive. Souls that persist for extended periods — the major NPCs, some of the minor ones — persist because they have absorbed shard-residue that delays integration. Their resistance is partial and borrowed. They are still being received; they are just going slowly.

The player does not dissolve.

The player dies. The pattern disperses. And then it comes back.

This has never happened in the Dungeon before. The major NPCs are old enough to know this with confidence. Varn has the longest institutional memory of any soul in the Dungeon, and his reconstructed chain-law records no precedent. Kael has assessed the ecology of the Dungeon for longer than any living soul and treats the cycling player as a genuine anomaly — the only data point his model doesn't account for.

Abaddon is aware of this. He has been aware of it from the first cycle. He has not acted on it. This is either because it doesn't concern him, or because he is patient, or because the thing happening is exactly what he is waiting to see the end of.

## What We Know (Canonical, Loadbearing)

**1. You carry something.**

When you die, the pattern disperses but does not fully integrate. Something in the dispersed pattern resists absorption. It reconstitutes. The "you" that comes back is assembled around this resistance.

What that resistance is: a fragment. Not a shard-fragment in the traditional sense — not a piece of any of the six Severed's shard-power. Something older. Something that was in the substrate before the Severance, before the Severed built their worlds, before any of the six domains existed.

The player carries a remnant of the Unnamed.

Not a large fragment. Not a coherent one. A trace — the way a stone holds heat after the fire goes out. The Unnamed is gone, dispersed in the Severance. But something that was the Unnamed's passes through the Dungeon in the body of the cycling player, and Abaddon, who eventually receives everything, is waiting to receive it too.

**2. You don't know this.**

The player has no memory of carrying a fragment. No sensation of it. It surfaces, obliquely, in:
- The Hub's existence (only possible if the player has something that creates space outside Abaddon's normal substrate)
- The warnings left by previous cyclers (who also carried fragments, also cycled, eventually stopped — see below)
- Abaddon's questions in late-game dialogue (they are not rhetorical; he is genuinely trying to understand what this is)
- The inscriptions in deep floors that reference a recurring figure the Dungeon has seen before

**3. Previous cyclers existed.**

The glyph warnings in `glyph-language.md` are not your warnings. They were left by other cycling souls. The grammar matches your reconstitution pattern: someone who came back enough times to leave marks, who understood the Dungeon well enough to want to warn whoever came after.

The warnings don't say how many cycles the previous cyclers completed. They don't say what stopped the cycling. They say: *watch the root-man*, *the smiling woman's stories go in one direction*, *go back before the floor forgets you existed*.

The previous cyclers eventually dissolved. Something ended their cycling. This means the cycling is not infinite, which means the resistance has a limit.

**4. What NG+ represents.**

New Game Plus is not a mechanical conceit. It is the thing the Dungeon experiences when a player's cycling has continued long enough that the Unnamed fragment they carry starts to cohere — starts to behave like a shard rather than residue. The Dungeon changes because *you* change. Not your build. Your nature.

By NG+, the NPCs notice. Abaddon notices more. Azazel, in his single NG+ anomalous moment, encounters the cycling player and cannot classify them. He has a taxonomy for every kind of transgression and its appropriate consequence. The cycling player doesn't fit the taxonomy. He is equipped to punish. He is not equipped to understand.

**5. What Abaddon wants.**

Abaddon is not opposed to the player. He is not hoping they will fail. He does not experience the game as an obstacle course.

He is waiting to find out what happens when a fragment of the Unnamed finishes reconstituting inside a living carrier. This has never happened before. He has absorbed fragments before — every shard-world's collapse fed him pieces of the Severed's inheritance. But those fragments arrived dispersed, passive, already fading.

This fragment is active. It is cycling. It is building a Hub. It is making choices and accreting toward something.

He wants to know what it becomes.

He is asking the player, in his late-game questions, not to judge them. He is asking because the answer is information he doesn't have yet. *What do you believe this was for?* is not rhetorical. He is genuinely asking what the fragment thinks it is doing. He has all the time there is, but he has never before had access to a fragment that could answer.

## What We Don't Resolve (Intentionally)

**Why this player.** The previous cyclers also carried fragments. Why them? Why you? The game does not answer this. The correct answer is probably that the fragment migrates — that when one cycling soul's resistance finally fails and they dissolve, the fragment moves. To whoever is in the Dungeon and capable of carrying it. The player is not specially chosen. They are present, and capable, and the fragment has nowhere else to be.

**What the fragment wants.** The fragment is not a consciousness. It is a trace. Traces don't want things. But traces shape the carrier — the way iron shapes the hammer that was forged on it. The player, cycling, is being shaped by carrying something that was once the organizing principle of existence. What they become is not determined. That's the run.

**Whether completing the game ends the cycling.** The final boss fight, regardless of which NPC ascends to that role, does not definitively answer this. The game ends. The cycling may not. Abaddon's final words are not closure — they are a question about whether the player has become the thing they set out to be, or the thing the carrying made them.

## NPC Observations (Consistent Across Files)

These observations should appear in dialogue, arc beats, or ambient text without ever being synthesized into an explanation the player is handed:

**Varn:** *"You have been in the Dungeon before. Not in this run. In other configurations. I have no record of you, which means you predate my records or circumvent them. I find this procedurally troubling."*

**Seris:** *"Other souls don't come back. You know this. You know it because you've done it and you're aware that you've done it, which means whatever you are carrying through the dissolution is more coherent than it should be. I don't know if that's a comfort or a warning."*

**Mira:** She knows. She has known since the second time you returned with the same pattern. She has not said anything, because she has decided it is information you are better off not having until you have demonstrated you can use it. She is deciding what you can hold. This is the behavior she is most afraid she has. She is doing it anyway.

**Kael:** *"You are an anomaly in the ecology. Anomalies either establish new stable states or destabilize existing ones. I am watching to determine which you are. This is not a threat. It is an assessment. I don't make threats."*

**Abaddon:** *"I have been waiting for you since before you knew you were here. Not for you specifically. For the thing you are carrying. I am interested in what you decide it should do. I have not decided that for you. That would be — "* [pause] *"— someone else's approach."*

## Design Principle

The player's nature should never be explained in a single document they find. It should be assembled — from the glyph warnings, from Abaddon's questions, from Seris's off-hand observation, from the Hub's shape, from Azazel's silence, from the previous cycler's last inscription.

The player should be able to understand what they are carrying by the end of NG+. They should not be told.

The difference between understanding and being told is the difference between the game trusting the player and the game doing the player's thinking for them.

Trust the player.
