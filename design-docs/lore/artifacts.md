# Dungeoneer — Artifact Lore

*Legendary artifacts are not well-made weapons. They are fragments of a dead god-killer's divine inheritance, crystallized into physical form. When you equip an item from The Bound, you are channeling a piece of Solen's shard. Build around one domain's artifacts and you are effectively reconstructing one Severed's vision of the world inside your own body.*

*Each entry below is the flavor text the player reads when they identify a legendary.*

---

## How to Recognize Domain Artifacts

Before a legendary is identified, its domain origin is visible in its appearance:

- **Bound artifacts** are grey-silver metal with fine chain links, clasps, and bracket fittings. They look functional and deliberate. Nothing is decorative.
- **Pyre artifacts** are dark metal veined with orange-red, or flame-colored gems set in blackened housings. They are warm to the touch before they are equipped.
- **Veil artifacts** are deep blue, crystalline, or carry a faint iridescence. They do not look quite the same from two different angles.
- **Canopy artifacts** are organic — carved from living wood that is still alive, grown rather than forged, set with amber and rough-cut green stones.
- **Pale artifacts** are white or pale grey with fracture lines running through them. They look like they have survived something. The fractures do not affect their structural integrity.
- **Endless artifacts** are dark — almost black, or deep burgundy — with a surface that seems to absorb light rather than reflect it. They have been fed. You can feel it.

## The Bound — Artifacts of Law

*Items of consequence and mutual binding. Equipment that applies effects to both the wearer and the struck simultaneously.*

---

**The Weight of Witness**
*Ring — Legendary*

> Solen believed that law without witness is preference. Every contract in The Bound required a witness who had no stake in the outcome — someone who could see clearly because they had nothing to gain from seeing incorrectly.
>
> This ring was worn by the chief witness of The Bound's highest court. It carries the impression of every contract it observed. You can feel them all, and they hold.
>
> *When you take a hit, your attacker takes it too. You chose to be here. So did they.*

---

**Third Warning**
*Weapon — Legendary*

> The chain-laws required three warnings before consequence. Not two, not four. Three. One to inform. One to confirm. One to establish that ignorance was no longer a defense.
>
> Varn enforced this standard without exception. This blade counts for you. By the third strike, it has informed, confirmed, and established.
>
> *Your third consecutive hit against the same target deals judgment damage. Reset on target change.*

---

**The Adjudicator's Burden**
*Chest Armor — Legendary*

> He who carries enforcement carries the weight of every decision he has made against someone who deserved better. Not someone who was innocent — someone who was guilty, and also suffering, and also deserving of something the law did not provide.
>
> Varn wore armor like this for forty cycles. He said the weight was the point. An enforcer who couldn't feel the cost had stopped being an enforcer and started being something else.
>
> *Damage you take is reduced and delayed. You still take it. The delay is the cost of the reduction.*

---

**Mutual Binding**
*Amulet — Legendary*

> The chain-contracts of The Bound made agreements real and visible. You bound yourself to an oath, and the chain made it permanent. What you promised, you carried. What you were owed, the other party carried.
>
> This amulet was worn during the signing of The Bound's foundational contract — the agreement that made all other agreements possible. It still holds both sides of something.
>
> *Status effects you apply to enemies also apply to you at half strength. Buffs you gain also apply to nearby allies.*

---

## The Pyre — Artifacts of Renewal

*Items that strengthen as the wielder takes damage. The closer to death, the more power available.*

---

**What the Ash Kept**
*Weapon — Legendary*

> Ciara believed destruction that leaves nothing behind failed its purpose. Every burning in The Pyre was supposed to leave seeds — knowledge compressed into a form that survived the fire and grew from the ash.
>
> This blade was forged at the peak of The Pyre's civilization, when the cycle was still being honored. It has seen many fires. It still holds what they burned.
>
> *On kill, absorbs a fragment of the enemy's damage type. The absorbed type applies to your next three strikes.*

---

**Keeper's Last Ruling**
*Chest Armor — Legendary*

> She ruled against the burning of the living archive. She could not enforce the ruling. The archive burned anyway. She stood in the ash afterward and tried to remember what had been in it.
>
> The fire that took the archive soaked into this armor instead of the air. It has been hot ever since. It does not forgive.
>
> *When you take damage that would kill you, instead survive at 1 HP and gain a burst of fire damage on your next attack. Once per floor.*

---

**The Cycle Wants**
*Ring — Legendary*

> Ciara said: the version of the world that comes after destruction is what we want, and you cannot have it without the destruction. She was not wrong. She was not accounting for what happens when you want the destruction and nothing else.
>
> This ring was taken from a fire-advocate who had forgotten the second half of the sentence. It still carries the wanting. It has been separated from the context that would have made the wanting useful.
>
> *Your damage scales with your missing health. At full health, the ring is dormant.*

---

**Seed Form**
*Shield — Legendary*

> Knowledge in The Pyre was carried in people, not stone — compressed to its essential form, preserved through its holder, released when the holder was gone.
>
> This shield carries something that survived the final cycle's fire. It has been waiting for the right moment to release. The shield has been deciding for a very long time what the right moment is.
>
> *On shield break, releases stored damage as an explosion centered on you. The shield regenerates after 30 seconds.*

---

## The Veil — Artifacts of Selfhood

*Items that operate through deception and misperception. They alter what enemies believe is happening.*

---

**The Useful Shape**
*Chest Armor — Legendary*

> Thessaly said that a self which functions is more real than a self which is merely true. She meant this philosophically. This armor applies it physically.
>
> It was worn by one of Mira's predecessors — a shaper who worked with soldiers, helping them construct combat identities that could sustain what their actual identities couldn't. The armor remembers all of the shapes it has held.
>
> *Enemies target the position you were in 0.5 seconds ago rather than the position you are in. Attacks that miss because of this do no damage.*

---

**What She Chose**
*Ring — Legendary*

> She looked in every mirror until she found one she could accept. The mirror agreed. The shard made the agreement real. This is how it worked in The Veil: the story you told was as real as the story that was told about you, and both were as real as the facts.
>
> This ring carries the impression of a choice that was made completely — no ambivalence, no residual version of the previous self. Whoever wore this knew exactly who they were. You can feel the certainty through the metal.
>
> *One of your active abilities creates a phantom duplicate of you that attracts enemy targeting for 3 seconds.*

---

**The Necessary Distance**
*Boots — Legendary*

> The war of selves was invisible and total. Two truths competing for the same space, neither willing to yield, each one unmade by the other's presence. Mira spent her last years finding the distances at which incompatible truths could coexist — the gap that let both parties continue.
>
> These boots were made for that work. They maintain the necessary distance between what you are and what something else believes you are.
>
> *Your dodge creates a short-duration phantom at your previous location. Enemies will attack the phantom.*

---

**The War of Selves**
*Weapon — Legendary*

> When two truths are incompatible, reality follows the one held more completely. She said this not as a threat but as a description of how The Veil worked. The war had no armies. It had conviction.
>
> This blade was carried by someone who lost the war — who encountered a truth held more completely than their own and found their self dissolving around the edges. The blade remembers what it felt like to be on the losing side. It prefers winning.
>
> *Attacks inflict Doubt — a debuff that causes the target to perceive threats from the wrong direction for 4 seconds.*

---

## The Canopy — Artifacts of Nature

*Items that scale with run duration. Weak at the start, formidable by the end.*

---

**Patient Root**
*Weapon — Legendary*

> It was planted with intention and grew without supervision. In The Canopy, the things that survived long enough to become formidable were not the things that started strongest. They were the things that kept growing while everything else stopped.
>
> This weapon has been in the Dungeon long enough to remember what it was planted for. Whether it still remembers is a question you will answer over the course of carrying it.
>
> *Damage increases by 2% per minute of run time. Resets each run.*

---

**The Gardener's Argument**
*Chest Armor — Legendary*

> Kael said balance requires a gardener. This armor agrees with him. The longer you wear it, the more thoroughly it has incorporated your biology into its own structure. By the end of a long run, it has grown into you the way roots grow into stone: slowly, thoroughly, and in a way that cannot be reversed without damage to both.
>
> He would consider this a successful argument.
>
> *Armor rating increases by 1% per minute of run time. At 30 minutes, begins to apply minor regeneration.*

---

**What Flourished**
*Amulet — Legendary*

> Six cycles of genuine flourishing. Kael watched it and did nothing because the flourishing was real and he did not want to be the reason it ended. He regrets this decision in the specific way that someone regrets doing the right thing for the wrong reason.
>
> This amulet was carved from the heartwood of the tallest tree in the region Kael managed — the one that grew during those six cycles and was the first thing consumed when the collapse came. It carries the memory of having been the tallest.
>
> *Killing blow on an elite enemy grants a stacking damage buff that persists until you take lethal damage.*

---

**Earned Hierarchy**
*Boots — Legendary*

> In The Canopy, you wore your place. You did not inherit it. Every position in the hierarchy was the result of something demonstrated — strength, endurance, cunning, the particular fitness that the ecology required at that point in time.
>
> These boots were earned. You can feel it in how they fit — they adjust to you over time, learning your stride, committing to it. By the end of a run they have made a judgment about what you are.
>
> *Movement speed increases by 0.5% per floor descended. Bonus resets to base on death.*

---

## The Pale — Artifacts of Preservation

*Items that freeze states — debuffs, cooldowns, enemy actions, status effects.*

---

**The Held Moment**
*Ring — Legendary*

> Vael believed you could hold a beautiful thing indefinitely without it aging. He was almost right. He could hold it. It did not age. It also did not grow, or change, or become anything other than what it was the moment it was held.
>
> This ring was worn during the crystallization of what Vael considered the most perfect moment he had witnessed. He held it for the rest of his existence. When his shard collapsed, it was the last thing to go.
>
> *On activation, freezes all active status effects on you in place for 6 seconds — pausing their duration without removing their benefit.*

---

**Preservation Vessel**
*Chest Armor — Legendary*

> Perfect container. Something was held in here for longer than the collapse. The armor outlasted its contents, outlasted the civilization that made it, outlasted the shard that gave it meaning. It still holds the shape of what it kept.
>
> Whatever was inside has gone. The holding posture remains. When you wear this, the armor begins assessing what else it might preserve.
>
> *When you would take lethal damage, instead suspend the killing blow for 2 seconds. During this suspension you cannot die. After 2 seconds, the damage resolves.*

---

**Vael's Regret**
*Weapon — Legendary*

> He froze what he loved until it couldn't move. Not because he wanted to trap it — because he was afraid of what would happen to it if he let it continue. He has been holding it frozen ever since, not quite able to begin the process of release.
>
> This blade carries the specific quality of that failure: things struck with it are held in place not by force but by the weapon's conviction that moving is more dangerous than staying still.
>
> *Strikes apply Suspension — a stacking debuff. At 3 stacks, the target is frozen for 2 seconds.*

---

**The Empty Archive**
*Amulet — Legendary*

> It remembers everything. Not what was in it — the act of remembering, without content. The structure of knowledge, perfectly preserved, with no knowledge inside it.
>
> Vael built archives that lasted. The contents of the archives did not. What remains is the perfect memory of having once known things, which is one of the stranger forms of grief available to an inanimate object.
>
> *On floor entry, stores the current state of your cooldowns. Once per floor, you may restore all cooldowns to the stored state.*

---

## The Endless — Artifacts of Desire

*Items that feed on consumption. They grow stronger with kills, with mana drained, with health stolen.*

---

**First Hunger**
*Weapon — Legendary*

> Carath said yearning was the engine of everything worth anything. Love. Ambition. Creation. Grief. You cannot make something without wanting it to exist before it does. The Unnamed had removed the wanting, and Carath considered this an annihilation dressed as generosity.
>
> This weapon carries the first version of that argument — before it became policy, before it became civilization, before it became the thing that ate the civilization. When the wanting was still pointed at something.
>
> *Each kill increases damage by 3%. Bonus decays by 1% every 10 seconds without a kill.*

---

**What Was Taken**
*Chest Armor — Legendary*

> The Endless consumed itself soul by soul. Each one believing their hunger was the justified kind — the kind that produced rather than merely took. Some of them were right. Most of them were not. The armor does not distinguish.
>
> It has been here long enough to remember everything it has absorbed. Some of what it remembers surfaces in unexpected ways.
>
> *On being struck, stores 10% of damage taken as charge. Release charge on your next attack as bonus damage.*

---

**The Appetite That Learned**
*Amulet — Legendary*

> A hunger that remembers what it ate develops preferences. The thing in The Hollow Courts that consumed everything was not, by the end, indiscriminate. It had been consuming long enough to know what it wanted. This is not reassuring. It means the hunger had time to become something more than hunger.
>
> This amulet carries the preference. It has decided what it wants. If your build happens to align with what it wants, the alignment is very powerful. If it doesn't, you will feel the misalignment.
>
> *After killing 20 enemies of the same type, gain a permanent 5% damage bonus against that type.*

---

**Carath's Answer**
*Ring — Legendary*

> He believed the Unnamed had removed the wanting, and called this an annihilation. He was making a specific philosophical point: that desire is not a flaw to be corrected but the precondition for anything worth having.
>
> He was not wrong. He was also not accounting for what desire becomes when nothing checks it. This ring gives the wanting back. The wanting does not come with instructions.
>
> *Life steal on all attacks. The life steal percentage increases as your current health decreases. At full health: 2%. At half health: 8%. At low health: 15%.*

---

## Cross-Domain Notes

*For the player who has been paying attention:*

A full six-domain artifact build is not possible. The domains are philosophically incompatible. A set of Bound artifacts and a set of Endless artifacts will not harmonize — the chain-logic and the consumption-logic conflict at the shard level. The artifacts will still function individually, but the resonance bonuses that reward committing to a domain will not apply.

This is not a balance decision. This is lore made mechanical. The Severed didn't agree on what came after. Their shards remember.

The most powerful builds are built around one domain's philosophy, taken to its logical extreme. This is what the game is asking you to consider: which of the six answers to *"what was wrong with the old world?"* do you believe? Your artifact set is your answer.
